# Paso 1 · Investigar y escribir el guion (prompt original del usuario)

> Fuente: guía "Claude Code + YouTube Shorts" (Danny Why), aportada por el usuario el 2026-10-30.
> Adaptación vigente: ver `Projects/VideoEditor/Guia-produccion.md` en el cerebro (idioma, duración).

Research the internet and write one original YouTube Shorts script about a surprising fact or an interesting question. The video will use simple 2D animations and should be 20–30 seconds long, with around 50–70 words of narration. Look for unusual questions, surprising explanations, everyday mysteries, or strange facts about science, animals, history, the human body, space, or engineering. For example, "Why don't we throw trash into volcanoes?" works because it suggests a simple solution and makes people wonder why we don't use it. Use that kind of curiosity, but find a different topic. Research several ideas before choosing the strongest one. Choose something that can be explained clearly in 20–30 seconds, has a surprising answer or consequence, is easy to show through simple 2D animations, and makes sense without background knowledge. Each time I send this prompt, choose a new topic and explanation. Check the ideas already visible in this conversation and avoid repeating them or making slight variations of them. Vary the subject categories too. If you cannot access previous ideas, don't claim you checked them. Verify the central fact using reliable sources, preferably original research, universities, museums, government agencies, or established scientific organizations, and cross-check the explanation where possible. Do not invent facts, numbers, quotes, or consequences, and keep uncertainty where it matters. If you cannot browse the internet, say so rather than pretending you researched the topic. Start the script immediately with a specific question or surprising statement, without a greeting or introduction. Build curiosity by explaining what would happen, why the obvious answer is wrong, or what makes the situation unusual, then deliver a clear, satisfying answer near the end. Build toward that answer without filler or dragging out the explanation. Use simple, natural, conversational English with short, connected sentences that sound good when spoken aloud. Every sentence should add useful information. Avoid generic hooks like "Did you know?", "This will blow your mind," or "You won't believe this." Avoid forced suspense, exaggerated claims, AI-sounding phrases, and calls to action. Use original wording and do not copy another creator's script. Choose the best idea yourself without asking me to pick a topic. In your response, give me a one-sentence description of the idea, the complete voiceover ready to record without scene directions, the narration word count, and links to the sources that support the explanation.
